# Pilot learning journeys

## Explorer to first lesson

**Decisions:** discovery flight, certificate path, medical timing, budget, school/instructor, training frequency.  
**Use:** discovery-flight questions, training budget calculator, school/CFI scorecard.  
**Escalate:** medical history to an AME before making commitments; certificate/eligibility edge cases to a CFI/FAA source.

## Student — foundations

**Goals:** consistent schedule, ground/flight integration, effective lesson preparation, accurate debriefing.  
**Use:** curriculum Modules 1–5; lesson brief/debrief; progress tracker.  
**Milestone:** can explain core concepts, locate primary sources, and connect them to CFI demonstrations.

## Student — pre-solo

**Goals:** local procedures, endorsements, aircraft knowledge, judgment, stabilized habits.  
**Use:** instructor-owned readiness plan; airport/airspace/radio modules; personal-minimum draft.  
**Boundary:** the CFI alone determines endorsement/readiness. A generic checklist cannot substitute.

## Student — cross-country

**Goals:** weather interpretation, route/fuel/performance planning, diversion, lost procedures, risk management.  
**Use:** Modules 6–10; PAVE/5P worksheet; nav-log review prompts.  
**Boundary:** exact aircraft data, current operational information, and CFI review control.

## Checkride candidate

**Goals:** ACS coverage, scenario reasoning, document/endorsement readiness, risk articulation.  
**Use:** ACS loop in `curriculum.md`; mock oral prompts; knowledge-gap log.  
**Boundary:** do not guarantee eligibility or outcome. The instructor and DPE/FAA process control.

## First 90 days after certification

Create a proficiency plan, not just a currency plan:

- Week 1: debrief checkride learning; identify weak tasks.
- Month 1: fly with a CFI on one weak area and one new scenario.
- Month 2: supervised or conservative cross-country expansion.
- Month 3: night, weather-avoidance, unfamiliar-airport, or passenger-risk refresher as appropriate.
- Set personal-minimum review dates and explicit reasons to tighten them.

## Renter and flying-club pilot

**Decisions:** checkout, operating rules, scheduling, damage responsibility, policy questions, currency/proficiency.  
**Use:** rental agreement worksheet; FBO/club checklist; non-owned insurance question list.  
**Boundary:** do not interpret a rental contract or policy; route to counsel or licensed representative.

## Recommended response questions

Ask only what changes the answer:

- What certificate/rating and training stage are you at?
- Which jurisdiction applies?
- What is your timeline and realistic weekly availability?
- What aircraft category/class and training environment?
- Is this learning/planning, or are you preparing for a current flight?
- Which exact source, aircraft document, or professional is available to verify the result?
