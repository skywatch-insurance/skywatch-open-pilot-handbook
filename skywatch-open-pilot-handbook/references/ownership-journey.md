## 1. Decide whether to own

Compare four models:

| Model | Best when | Main trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Rent | Low/moderate use, flexibility, limited capital | Availability, checkout/rules, less control |
| Flying club | Community and access matter | Shared governance and scheduling |
| Partnership | Higher access with shared fixed cost | Legal/relationship/exit complexity |
| Sole ownership | Mission control and availability justify cost/effort | Capital, maintenance, downtime, administration |

Use the mission worksheet and ownership calculator. Do not treat cost alone as the decision; include availability, dispatch reliability, training, maintenance ecosystem, and exit plan.

## 2. Define mission and constraints

Capture:

- occupants, baggage, typical and maximum legs;
- runway, elevation, temperature, terrain, and weather environment;
- day/night and VFR/IFR intentions consistent with qualifications;
- acquisition cash, monthly capacity, and unplanned-expense reserve;
- hangar/tie-down reality;
- maintenance and parts availability;
- transition-training and insurability questions;
- acceptable downtime and exit horizon.

Do not recommend an aircraft from brochure payload/range alone. Real equipped weight, condition, exact performance, pilot capability, and mission margins matter.

## 3. Build the team and budget

Potential roles: buyer's broker, independent A&P/IA, type expert, CFI/transition instructor, lender, aviation attorney, title/escrow provider, aviation CPA, and licensed insurance representative.

Budget layers:

- acquisition and tax/closing cash;
- immediate discrepancies/upgrades;
- fixed annual costs;
- variable hourly costs;
- scheduled reserves;
- unplanned reserve and downtime contingency.

## 4. Search and screen

Before travel or an offer, request enough information to reject poor fits:

- identity/model/year/serial and current location;
- total time and engine/prop times with definitions;
- useful load and equipment list;
- logbook continuity and digitized records;
- damage/incident/repair disclosure;
- inspection status and known discrepancies;
- ownership/title/lien handling;
- reason for sale and recent utilization;
- seller's proposed pre-buy access.

## 5. Pre-buy and records

The buyer chooses an independent inspector familiar with the type. Define scope, access, test flights/engine runs, records review, discrepancy classification, expansion authority, and reporting before work begins.

Review categories: identity and registration; logbook continuity; annual/100-hour status; AD research; major repairs/alterations and forms; life-limited items; engine/prop history; corrosion/damage evidence; avionics/equipment documentation; weight-and-balance history; recurring maintenance; unresolved discrepancies.

Boundary: only qualified professionals using current data can inspect, determine condition/airworthiness, or approve return to service.

## 6. Offer, closing, and delivery

Sequence professional work deliberately:

1. Mission and budget gate.
2. Preliminary insurance/transition questions.
3. Offer reviewed as appropriate, with inspection/records/title/financing contingencies.
4. Independent records/title/lien and pre-buy work.
5. Resolve accept/reprice/repair/exit decision with professionals.
6. Finalize financing, entity/tax/legal actions, insurance, registration, escrow, and delivery.
7. Preserve all signed documents and baseline records.

Do not recommend contract terms, entity structure, tax treatment, or policy coverage.

## 7. First 30 days

- Complete transition training and define conservative initial limitations.
- Inventory current aircraft documents, keys, equipment, subscriptions, and records.
- Establish maintenance contacts and a discrepancy/squawk workflow.
- Reconcile actual equipment and weight-and-balance documents.
- Build an inspections/AD/recurring-items calendar with the A&P/IA.
- Confirm hangar/tie-down, security, emergency equipment, and support contacts.
- Confirm policy/admin facts with the licensed representative after any changes.
- Capture baseline engine/operating data only through appropriate procedures.

## 8. Months 2–12

### Monthly

- Reconcile fuel/oil/maintenance/operating spending with budget.
- Review squawks and deferred decisions with the mechanic.
- Check proficiency, not merely legal currency.

### Quarterly

- Review mission fit, personal minimums, training needs, and reserve adequacy.
- Fly with a qualified instructor when expanding capability or recovering weak proficiency.

### Before annual and renewal

- Schedule early and define inspection scope.
- Assemble aircraft, pilot, utilization, training, storage, and modification changes.
- Review agreed value and requested limits/questions with the licensed representative.
- Update year-two cost and maintenance plan.

### Year-one retrospective

Compare planned vs. actual hours, cost, downtime, missions, training, and surprises. Decide whether to keep, modify, partner, change mission, or sell only after separating one-time acquisition effects from ongoing ownership reality.
